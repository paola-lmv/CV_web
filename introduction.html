Why Consulting ?

Consulting appeals to me because it would allow me to work on diverse and intellectually stimulating projects where I could leverage my broad and versatile academic background. Breaking down complex problems and solving them efficiently through structured logic was a key skill I developed during my academic background.

Why Sustainability ?

Sustainability is a topic that has always resonated deeply with me. I’ve always felt close to nature through my passion for animals, and for me, protecting it means defending sustainable development. As I grew up, I became more aware of the environmental tensions and inequalities linked to it. Now, sustainability is no longer just about protecting nature, it’s about ensuring a future where society as a whole can live and grow in a livable environment.

Over time, I’ve realized how complex this issue is, connecting science, economics, and social justice. That’s what makes it so fascinating to me, sustainability is not only about environmental awareness, but also about how we can rethink our systems to make them fairer and more resilient.

I also believe this topic is still underestimated, and that not enough concrete actions are taken. That’s why I want to dedicate my work to something meaningful, like contributing to this cause.

This belief has shaped my academic, professional and personal choices. In my daily life, I try to make conscious decisions, limiting air travel, buying second-hand clothes, and staying informed through readings and sustainability podcasts. Academically, I have focused my studies on sustainability and climate-related topics: from pursuing an engineering degree with special modules (Life Cycle Assessment, Carbon Accounting), to studying forest sciences during my Erasmus exchange in Finland, and now exploring climate change science and finance in my master’s at Imperial College London.

Professionally, I reinforced this commitment during my summer internship at Citepa, a non-profit mandated by the French Ministry of Ecology to assess national greenhouse gas emissions. There, I analyzed the carbon footprint of France’s digital infrastructure, gaining valuable insight into how data and policy interact in the transition to a low-carbon economy.
